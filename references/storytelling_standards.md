# Storytelling Standards

## The spine
- **Central question** — one sentence; the film is its investigation.
- **Hook** — first 15 s: concrete image, claim, or contradiction. Never a
  table-of-contents ("today we will explore…").
- **Progression** — each act partially answers and opens the next question.
  Curiosity gaps close only when a bigger one opens.
- **Conflict** — stakes, reversals, human consequences; explicit, not implied.
- **Payoff** — the answer, the meaning, the emotional landing. End on one
  strong beat; no summary-of-the-film endings.

## Pacing
- Alternate dense and airy: after ~90 s of continuous dense narration, one
  visual-only beat (music + image, no voice).
- Strong material earns long takes; weak material gets cut, not decorated.
- One fact, one place: don't restate what the picture shows.

## Voice ( narration )
- Written for the ear: read aloud test on every line.
- Short sentences, concrete nouns, active voice, one idea per sentence.
- Banned patterns: "delve", "tapestry", "rich history", "in today's
  fast-paced world", "it's important to note", rhetorical-question padding,
  triple-adjective stacks.
- Hedging only where `fact_check.md` demands it.

## Emotional honesty
- Music and imagery may intensify what the facts support — never manufacture
  emotion the story hasn't earned (no sad-cello over a statistic that doesn't
  involve people).

## Structural review
The Story-editor's pass (`modules/story-editor.md`) is mandatory before
storyboarding; its notes and accepted changes go in `script/story_notes.md`.
